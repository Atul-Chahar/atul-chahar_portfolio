How I work

  1. 1

    A model proposes.

    Agents draft the plan, and another agent tries to break it. DENO runs eight of them, with a critic that argues against every startup idea before it gets a verdict.

  2. 2

    Plain code verifies.

    Nothing a model says is trusted on its own. KILLSWITCH checks every containment step against recorded CloudTrail facts before it is allowed to run.

  3. 3

    A person approves.

    Anything irreversible waits for a human. In Foreman, auto-approving a dangerous action is a compile-time false, not a setting someone can flip.

Outspoken

A patient on a ventilator or someone with ALS is often awake and clear-headed but cannot say “I am in pain”. Outspoken reads their eyes through the front camera, lets them pick replies or say anything, and speaks it in a natural voice, all on the phone. I shipped 14 merged pull requests: the designed screens, 17 languages, the patient profile, Hindi and English switching, a Kokoro voice and safer model loading. It reached the semi-final of the Tata Imagination Challenge 2026.

DENO

Most AI tools will happily build a landing page for a bad idea. DENO asks first whether the idea should exist. Research, critic, strategy and decision agents loop and challenge each other: they size the market from the bottom up, find competitors with sources you can click, and map the communities where the first users are. Only an idea that survives moves on to a 30-day content engine that writes in the founder’s voice and scores every post for ban risk. It placed 2nd at the Lyzr Agentathon.

Kavach

The pitch opened with a real call: a stranger said my father had sent money, then a fake bank alert for ₹30,000 arrived, then a request to return ₹27,000. Kavach listens for those patterns during a call and warns you while it is still happening. Everything runs on the phone, and the internet permission is stripped from the manifest so that can be proved. One script re-measures all 31 claims in the README. Second runner-up, student track, iQOO Hackathon 2026 in Bengaluru, with Team Smoke Test.

Kotlin, on-device LLM, about 13,000 lines and 136 tests

KILLSWITCH

Push an AWS key to GitHub by mistake and bots find it in about five minutes. AWS quarantines the key, but it does not shut down what the attacker started. KILLSWITCH catches the leak, reads CloudTrail across regions to see what the key did, has a sandboxed model propose a containment plan, checks every line of that plan against the audit facts in plain Python, and waits for an operator before it touches anything. I designed the architecture; Gyan Ranjan built the approval console and Krishna Chaudhary the attacker script. Built for First Commit by WeMakeDevs and AWS.

Built with
Python, AWS CDK, React, 404 tests

Foreman

Foreman hands issues to a bounded swarm of AI implementers, each on its own branch inside a sandbox, then merges their work through a serial queue with a reviewer stage. A risk-tier policy stops anything irreversible at a human gate, and state lives in SQLite so a crashed run resumes instead of losing work. Parallel speed, serial safety. Built on TrueFoundry’s open-source TrueForge harness for the Agent Harness Hackathon.

JavaScript, TrueForge, MCP, SQLite, 118 tests

Kaagaz

Scheme finders tell a student what they are eligible for. Kaagaz tells them whether they are already too late. It walks the chain of certificates a scholarship needs in a FalkorDB graph, adds up the legal processing time for each, and names the document to apply for today, with a government source for every rule. A caseworker agent acts only after approval, and a watcher agent re-checks the rules against the live government pages. Built for Graph Hacks by WeMakeDevs and FalkorDB.
